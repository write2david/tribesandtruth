:::tip[Overview]{icon="sun"}
Salvation happens via the Gospel.

The Tribes expand the definition of "the Gospel" (and partially re-define the Gospel) in many ways, compared to what Scripture teaches.

The reason they do this is because they conflate the **root** of the Gospel (faith) with the **fruit** of the Gospel (a changed life). See [The Upside Down Tree](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1dsy4d5ATrpShBE9S_UJgzD1Ggx2JOYLCw3x6TvV2gqI/edit?usp=sharing).

Further, they specify that specific fruit (and not always biblically-required fruit) is part of that root.

It's a lot of things beyond the good news of Jesus loves you enough that He died to personally offer you reconciliation with God through faith in Himself.

They also conflate the "Messiah" (Jesus/Yahshua) with "the Body of Messiah" (the Tribes). Surrendering to Messiah is considered the same surrendering to the Tribes. Being baptized into Jesus is the same as being baptized into the Tribes. The act of making a commitment to the Messiah is also a commitment to the Tribes. Forsaking worldly possessions to follow Jesus means giving all your possessions to the Tribes. Entrusting your entire life to Jesus means entrusting your entire life to the Tribes' leadership.

## Response

The Tribes believe they are worthy of being God's special people...

...because they've made difficult psychological choices...

...like giving away all their possessions and letting the Tribes' leadership have complete authority over them...

...which qualifies them to receive salvation.


Incidentally, from the Tribes' perspective, becoming like Jesus is actually easier to accomplish than in Christianity, because the Tribes view Jesus as less Divine than Christianity does.


The overall effect is:

1) The uniqueness of the Savior is decreased (compared to biblical Christianity)

2) The requirements for gaining salvation is increased (compared to biblical Christianity)

3) The natural human nature of people is less fallen/sinful/helpless, and more capable of following God* (compared to biblical Christianity).


*To fulfill the requirements for salvation: fallen, unsaved people have the ability and responsibility to make significant changes to their own foundational choices & behavior (including giving up their desires, possessions, and self-will). This differs from Christianity, which does not believe that unsaved people are able (and often not even wanting) to accomplish major steps of biblical transformation ("sanctification") prior to salvation.

The Twelve Tribes do not teach self-forsaking, giving up of one's possessions and a common life with other believers is the fruit of a person having been saved by God's grace, but instead teach it is the cause and condition of salvation itself.

In contrast, the Bible teaches salvation is a supernatural, unconditional free gift that is given to God's people as an expression of God's love, because they are powerless to bring it about themselves on an account of being spiritually dead in sins and estranged from God (Romans 3:10, 3:24, 4:16, 5:10, 6:23. )  God provides the faith (Eph. 2:8-9) to believe the entire gospel - which is the whole truth about who God is, who mankind is, mankind's sin, and the truth about the person and work of Jesus. The Bible does not teach people must cooperate with God, meet conditions or prerequisites or perform works to bring about their salvation. The Bible teaches people to believe God, by looking away from themselves to another person - Jesus. A person is born again when God Himself quickens their dead spirit (Eph. 2:1-3), presents them with the truth in God's Word, provides the supernatural faith to believe His Word and the good news about Jesus Christ (Eph 2:8-9) and then seals them with God's spirit upon believing (Eph. 1:13).

This is something supernatural and real that is done to a person who cannot do it themselves. It comes from God, freely. The fruit is then a person desiring to lay down their lives and serve their brothers and sisters. The Twelve Tribes teach the Biblical fruit of salvation is in fact the self-willed condition of salvation.
