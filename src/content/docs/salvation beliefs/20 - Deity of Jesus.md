:::tip[Overview]{icon="sun"}
- Some members of the Tribes will say that Jesus ("Yahshua") is God incarnate and will affirm the traditional Christian perspective on the Trinity.

- Other members will say that Jesus is not God. And others aren't sure.

- The official doctrine of the Tribes is that Jesus was conceived as a pure/sinless human (similar to the first man, Adam) and then became a container for Divinity.

- This is an uncommon view typically known as [Dynamic Monarchianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoptionism)).

- They believe the Trinity is not that Father, Son, and Holy Spirit but is instead the Father, Word, and Holy Spirit. They believe that “the Word” is God and that the Word was united with “the man Yahshua” who was just human.

:::

Answers to Possible Rebuttals
 

Philippians 2:5-8
	“Your attitude should be the same as that of Christ Jesus:
Who, being in very nature God, did not conside equality with God something to be grasped, but
made Himself nothing, taking the very nature of a servant, being made in human likeness. And being
found in appearance as a man, He humbled Himself and became obedient to death – even death on a cross!”

     	Yahshua/Jesus came from the Father and from Heaven.  The experience of being in Heaven with the Father is something incredible that we cannot imagine. And since Jesus Himself is God  (as this passage begins by saying), everything in heaven glorified Him, too. However, this is not something that He grasped, or “held fast.” This passage shows Him going from being worshipped in Heaven to living on this earth and being killed. The phrase “made Himself nothing” is literally translated “emptied Himself.”  He did not empty Himself of His deity (God cannot change ), but Yahshua emptied Himself of the things this passage talks about: not coming to rule as Master of the universe but to wash feet as a servant, not coming with the heavenly glory of God but having human likeness and appearance, not demanding worship as the Supreme Being and Creator of the universe but being humble, not being beyond life and death but actually dying – even on a cross!
	This is the God who loves us.




John 14:28
	“You heard that I said to you, ‘I go away, and I will come to you.’ If you loved Me, you would have 			rejoiced, because I go to the Father; for the Father is greater than I.’ ”

     	As seen above in Philippians 2:5-8, Jesus position on this earth was not like His position with the Father in Heaven. When He came to earth, He did not stop being God, but He emptied Himself and did not grasp that which He experienced as God in Heaven. He became a man, and Deity dwelt in a human body (Colossians 2:9). As a man, Jesus “was made a little lower than the angels” (Hebrews 2:9). A comparison to Jesus (on earth) and the Father can be found in the marriage relationship. Biblically, a husband is greater in position and authority than his wife. However, he is no different in nature – they are each 100% human.
	 So, in this passage Jesus was not denying that He was God. But as a man, he was born of a woman and born under the law in order to redeem those under the law (Galatians 4:4-5). Those who are saved are children of God (John 1:12), and “since the children have flesh and blood, he too shared in their humanity so that by His death He might destroy him who holds the power of death – that is, the devil ... He had to be made like His brothers  in every way, in order that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest in service to God, and that He might make atonement for the sins of the people. Because He Himself suffered when He was tempted, He is able to help those who are being tempted”  (Hebrew 2:14-18). On earth Jesus emptied Himself and did everything these passages say, and He did it as God for us!

•	1st John 5:7-8 reads: “FOR THERE ARE THREE THAT TESTIFY: THE SPIRIT, THE WATER AND THE BLOOD” 	(NIV).  Almost all translations (especially recent ones) include “Spirit,” “water,” and 	“blood.”

•	However, a few late versions of the old Latin Vulgate translation (from the 5th century A.D.) have 1st John 5:7 reading like this: “FOR THERE ARE THESE THREE THAT 	TESTIFY IN HEAVEN: THE FATHER, THE WORD, AND THE HOLY SPIRIT, AND THESE THREE ARE 	ONE.”  This rendering of the verse does not show up in any Greek manuscripts 	until the 16th century, and so it is obvious that the faulty Latin translation did not 	give an accurate portrayal of the autographs (autographs = the original Greek manuscripts).
•	“The reading [of Father, Word, and Holy Spirit] seems to have arisen in a 4th century Latin homily [sermon] in which the text was allegorized to refer to members of the Trinity. From there, it made its way into copies of the Latin Vulgate... The Trinitarian formula (known as the Comma Johanneum) made its way into the third edition of Erasmus’ Greek NT because of pressure from the Catholic Church.” 
•	The Greek version called the Textus Receptus (published by Erasmus in 1516, with the third edition appearing in 1522) included this wording beginning in the third edition. The Textus Receptus included many flaws, and from this text a number of English translations were made.
•	Webster’s Bible (1833) and Young’s Literal Translation (1898) have the wording of the Trinity, as does the King James Version (1611) and New King 	James Version (NT 1979). All of these were translated from the Textus Receptus.
•	The “Douay-Rheims Version” (OT 1610, NT 1582) was translated from the Latin Vulgate and reflects that wording in 1st John 5:7 (the “1899 American Edition” of Douay-Rheims places 1st John 5:7-8 in 1st John 4:20).
