:::tip[Overview]{icon="sun"}
- The Twelve Tribes Communities teach that water baptism ("immersion") is a necessary step for salvation. According to their own documents: "Baptism is not something we do after we are saved. Baptism is the actual event of salvation."

- This view is called "[baptismal regeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptismal_regeneration)." Other groups that hold to this belief include most Roman Catholics, Eastern Orthodox, Churches of Christ, and Mormons (as well as some Lutherans and Anglicans). The Twelve Tribes say that "there is no separation between baptism and salvation. They are simultaneous events."

- The majority of Protestants, and especially Evangelicals, reject baptismal regeneration. Instead, they adhere to "faith alone" for salvation and view baptism as a post-salvation act to publicly identify oneself with Christ.

- Adherents to baptismal regeneration often consider non-adherents to be missing an essential component of salvation and thus to hold to a false Gospel. Conversely, groups that reject baptismal regeneration consider the requirement of baptism to be an additional "work" and thus a false Gospel.

- However, the Tribes go one step further than most groups. They believe that a person's connection to the Tribes is viewed as the primary indicator of salvation. They therefore view water baptism as **also** the step of being "immersed" into the *Body of Messiah* (aka the Tribes). "This transfer of kingdoms, which is salvation itself, happens in baptism where we renounce and forsake our old life and possessions, calling upon Yahshua to save us, and then we entrust our lives into the hands of his body on earth."
:::

## Response

### Two Types of Baptism

- The Bible uses "baptism" to describe two different situations: being placed into the body of Christ (spiritual), and being immersed in water (physical).

  - SPIRITUAL BAPTISM: Christians baptize each other "with water" (Acts 1:5, Acts 10:47) but Jesus baptizes people "with the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 3:11, Luke 3:16, Acts 1:5). When believers are "baptized into Christ" (Romans 6:3, Galatians 3:27), it "saves" them (1 Peter 3:21). At that point, believers are "buried with Him in baptism" (Colossians 2:12, cf. Romans 6:4). Being "baptized into Christ" (Romans 6:3) also baptizes a person into His body (1 Corinthians 12:13).

  - PHYSICAL BAPTISM: Jesus baptizes people with the Holy Spirit, saving them and placing them into His body. In contrast, water baptism is done by humans (Matthew 3:11, Matthew 28:19, Acts 2:41, Acts 8:36–38). John the Baptist listed both kinds of baptism: "I baptize you with water, but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit!" (Mark 1:8, cf. Luke 3:16, John 1:33). 
  

- Could the passages that refer to water baptism also be referring to spiritual baptism, such that there is only one type of baptism in the Bible?

  - Scripture repeatedly affirms (see section below) that baptism happens by the act of believing, without any other required actions.
  
  - Further, there are numerous accounts in Scripture (see other section below) of people getting saved *without* getting baptized or getting baptized *after* getting saved.

  - Paul specifically said that his job of preaching the Gospel (by which many people were indeed saved) did *not* include baptizing people (1 Corinthians 1:17).

  - The Bible repeatedly explains baptism as happening after believing (not at the moment of believing) and so the two moments are separate (which is why they are usually listed separately, as in Acts 18:8).

### Faith Alone

The Bible teaches that salvation comes through faith alone, apart from any "works" (including the "work" of being baptized with water).

- Titus 3:5 — God saved us "not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy."

- Ephesians 2:8–9 — Salvation is "not a result of works."

- John 6:28-29 — Jesus specifically says that the only "work" required for salvation is "to believe in the one He has sent."

- Romans 4:5 — To the person "who does not work," this person's "faith is credited as righteousness."

- John 3:16 — The most famous verse in the Bible affirms that belief is the one stated requirement for salvation.

- Acts 16:31–33 — The Philippian jailer asked Paul and Silas: "Sirs, what is necessary for me to do so that I may be saved?" The response he received was: "Believe on the Lord Jesus and you will be saved." That's all that was necessary to do.

- 1 Corinthians 1:17 — "Christ did not send me to baptize but to preach the gospel." Paul considered the Gospel to be separate from baptism.

- Romans 1:16 — The Gospel is "the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes." The phrase "*everyone* who believes" includes those who believe without being baptized.


### Baptism is still important.

- The New Testament assumes believers will be baptized (Matthew 28:19) as an act of obedience and public identification with Christ. 

- The question here is narrower: is baptism a *requirement* for salvation, or a *response* to salvation already given through faith?

***

## Salvation Without Baptism (or After Baptism)

Scripture repeatedly provides examples of salvation happening *without* baptism, or baptism happening *after* salvation...

- **Abraham** (Genesis 15:6; Romans 4). Abraham was credited with righteousness through faith alone, without baptism. Paul treats Abraham's case as the pattern for how Christians are saved (Romans 4:23–24). Similarly, Jesus declares Zacchaeus to be saved because he is a "son of Abraham" (Luke 19:1–9).

- **The paralytic on a mat** (Matthew 9:1–7). Jesus forgave the paralytic "when Jesus saw their faith." There was no baptism in this situation, but rather forgiveness came by faith alone.

- **The sinful woman** (Luke 7:36–50). Jesus didn't tell this woman, "Come, be baptized." Instead, He said: "Your faith has saved you; go in peace." He sent her off with assurance of salvation and without baptizing her.

- **The thief on the cross** (Luke 23:39–43). Jesus tells the dying criminal, "Today you will be with me in paradise." Baptism did not occur in this situation.

- **Cornelius and his household** (Acts 10:44–48). After Paul preached the Gospel, the people of this household (1) received the Holy Spirit, (2) exalted God, and (3) spoke in tongues. Scripture says that each of three results are indicators that salvation has occurred (John 4:23–24, Mark 16:17, Acts 2:4, Romans 8:9, 1 Corinthians 12:7–11, 1 Corinthians 14:23). And as a consequence, the new Christians were *then* baptized.

## Addressing Rebuttals

### Mark 16:16

*"Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved; whoever does not believe will be condemned."*

Three points limit how much weight this verse can carry:

1. The condemnation clause names only unbelief — baptism is dropped from it entirely. Taken at face value, the verse doesn't say an unbaptized believer is condemned — only an unbeliever.

2. Most textual scholars consider Mark 16:9–20 a later addition, absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts, and so probably not part of the original inspired text of the Bible.

3. If this verse meant that unbaptized-yet-believing people aren't saved, then it would contrast with the many other passages listed above.


### Acts 2:38

*"Repent and be baptized ... for the forgiveness of your sins."*

The entire issue here is based on the preposition "for" (which comes from the Greek word *eis*). This Greek word can mean "in order to obtain," but it can also mean "because of" or "with reference to" something already true.

- Matthew 3:11 — John says he baptizes "for [*eis*] repentance." Baptism doesn't produce repentance; it responds to it.

- Matthew 12:41 / Luke 11:32 — the Ninevites "repented at [*eis*] the preaching of Jonah" — repentance came *in response to* the preaching, not because the preaching manufactured it.

Read that way, Acts 2:38 fits the same pattern: baptism marks a forgiveness already occurring, rather than causing it. This also fits Acts 10:44–48, where the Spirit is given before baptism.


### Acts 22:16

*"Get up and be baptized, and wash away your sins, calling on His name"*

The grammar is instructive: "be baptized" and "wash away" are both imperatives, while "arise" and "calling" are participles.

That structure allows the verse to be read as, "Rising up, be baptized, and wash away your sins *by calling on His name*" — tying the forgiveness of sins to calling on Christ's name, not to the act of baptism. This matches Romans 10:13: "Whoever calls on the name of the Lord will be saved."

(Not every English translation preserves this grammatical structure equally clearly.)


### 1 Peter 3:21

*"Baptism ... now saves you"*

- As discussed above, Peter is referring to baptism by the Holy Spirit, not water baptism.

- In the very same verse, he specifies that this isn't water-baptism, as it's "not" the kind of baptism that can provide "removal of dirt from the body" but rather it is "an appeal to God for a good conscience." The good conscience is due to forgiveness provided by the Gospel.

- The Bible doesn't discuss water baptism as "saving" people. Jesus, not water, is the source of salvation.
