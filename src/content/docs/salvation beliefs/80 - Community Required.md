## Response

You can live in community and give up your possessions and still not be saved because you haven't established a relationship with God through Jesus.

Living in community wasn't promoted as requirements for our salvation originally in the tribes.

  
Question : “did any christians in the book of acts have their own home ?”
Answer —> 

Yes, several Christians in the book of Acts owned or maintained their own homes, which often served as meeting places for the early church. 

While Acts 2:44-45 and 4:32-37 describe believers selling property and holding goods in common, this was a voluntary, situational response to poverty within the community—not an abolishment of private property ownership. 

Specific Examples of Homeowners in Acts:
Mary, mother of John Mark: She owned a home in Jerusalem large enough to hold a "many" people gathered for prayer (Acts 12:12).
Lydia: She was a dealer in purple cloth who offered her home in Philippi to Paul and his companions (Acts 16:15, 16:40).
Jason: Provided hospitality to Paul and Silas in Thessalonica (Acts 17:7).
Titius Justus: Lived in Corinth and opened his home for worship (Acts 18:7).
Philip the Evangelist: Owned a house in Caesarea where he hosted Paul (Acts 21:8).
Priscilla & Aquila: While they travel, they are recognized throughout the New Testament (including Acts 18) for hosting a church in their home.
Paul: Although a tentmaker, he maintained a rented home in Rome for two years, where he taught those who came to him (Acts 28:30). 

Early Christians "broke bread" in their homes, and early converts often made their homes available for assemblies as the church grew, usually gathering in the homes of members who had space.
