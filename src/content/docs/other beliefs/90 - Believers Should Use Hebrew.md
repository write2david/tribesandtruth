:::tip[Overview]{icon="sun"}
- The Tribes believe that Hebrew will be the language of Heaven \[is this only the belief of the member who told me, or is this the official belief of the Tribes?\]

- When someone joins the Tribes, they are given a Hebrew name.

- Hebrew is also used in the Tribes for terms such as Jesus, God, mother, father, etc.

- The Tribes use "Edah" to refer to the local church or "community."

- The Tribes repeatedly promote "Yahshua" as the only correct & appropriate name for Jesus.
:::

## Response

- The Bible doesn't command, or even demonstrate, the need for believers to use Hebrew.

<br>

***

## Explanation

1. **The Apostles Used Greek Names and Terminology**[cite: 1]
   When reading the New Testament, we observe that the Apostles used Greek (and not Hebrew) when referring to each other and to the Messiah. For example, God inspired the Apostle Paul to use only the Greek name for Jesus (and to use it often). Look at all the Greek names in Romans 16.

2. **Early Church Writings Did Not Use Hebrew Translations**[cite: 1]
   Hebrew translations were also not used in the early Church writings.

3. **The Twelve Tribes Originally Used English Only**[cite: 1]
   The Twelve Tribes went many years using English only[cite: 1]. Creating new Hebrew names for members, and using Hebrew for a few other terms, was not introduced until well after the Tribes were established as an organization[cite: 1].

4. **New Testament Names Shifted to Greek, Not Hebrew**[cite: 1]
   When people got renamed in the New Testament, it seems they got renamed to Greek names even if they had Hebrew names previously[cite: 1]. Examples of Jews receiving non-Hebrew names: Saul was called Paul (Acts 13:9), Simon was called Peter (Matthew 10:2), the Messiah was called Christ (John 4:25), the Bible translates Tabitha into Dorcas for us (Acts 9:36), Simeon was called Niger (Acts 13:1), and Jesus (a name from Hebrew) was called Justus (from Latin) in Colossians 4:11[cite: 1].

5. **Aramaic, Not Hebrew, Was the Common Spoken Language**[cite: 1]
   Hebrew wasn't spoken very much when Jesus was alive[cite: 1]. It had been replaced by Aramaic.[^1][cite: 1]

6. **Geographical Names Were Used Instead of Tribal Designations**[cite: 1]
   The Tribes refer to regions of the world by Tribal names[cite: 1]. Paul did not; he referred to them by geographical location, only[cite: 1].

7. **Early Believers Used Standard Greek Terms**[cite: 1]
   Believers in the early church used the word “Jesus” and the word “Christ” when talking to other believers (1 Clement 21:6)[cite: 1].

8. **"Edah" and Hebrew Church Terminology Are Absent from the New Testament**[cite: 1]
   The New Testament does not use the word "Edah" to refer to church or community[cite: 1]. And the New Testament word for "church" does not come from Hebrew[cite: 1].

9. **The New Testament Manuscripts Are Written Entirely in Koine Greek**
   The ultimate proof that early Christians did not adopt a "Hebrew-only" vocabulary is that the Holy Spirit inspired the entire New Testament to be written and preserved in Koine Greek—the universal language of the everyday public—rather than Hebrew. Even when writing to predominantly Jewish-Christian audiences (such as the Epistles of Hebrews, James, and Peter), the authors wrote in Greek rather than Hebrew.

10. **Hebrew Words Are Expressly Translated *Out* of Hebrew for the Reader**
    When New Testament writers do use an occasional Hebrew or Aramaic term, they routinely translate it into standard Greek for their readers. If early Christians were expected to adopt Hebrew terms, these translations would be completely unnecessary:
    - **"Immanuel"** is immediately translated: *"which means, 'God with us'"* (Matthew 1:23).
    - **"Rabboni"** is translated: *"which means 'Teacher'"* (John 20:16).
    - **"Cephas"** is translated: *"which is translated, Peter"* (John 1:42).
    - **"Messiah"** is translated: *"which is translated, Christ"* (John 1:41, John 4:25).
    - **"Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani"** (Jesus' cry on the cross) is translated into Greek for the reader (Mark 15:34).
    - **"Maranatha"** is translated into Greek as *"Our Lord, come!"* or *"Amen. Come, Lord Jesus"* in subsequent letters rather than keeping the Aramaic/Hebrew phrasing standard (1 Corinthians 16:22, Revelation 22:20).

11. **The Holy Spirit Miraculously Sanctified All Languages at Pentecost (Acts 2)**
    When the Church was born at Pentecost, the Holy Spirit did not cause the international crowds to supernaturally understand Hebrew. Instead, God performed a miracle where every person heard the gospel in their own native, pagan languages (*"we hear them declaring the wonders of God in our own tongues!"*—Acts 2:11). This established from Day One that God accepts worship in every human language and does not require a sacred linguistic switch to Hebrew.

12. **The Greek Septuagint (LXX) Was the Standard Bible of the Early Church**
    When Jesus, the Apostles, and early Christians quoted Old Testament scriptures, they overwhelmingly quoted from the Septuagint—the Greek translation of the Old Testament. They routinely used the Greek title ***Kyrios*** ("Lord") and ***Theos*** ("God") to translate YHWH/Elohim rather than insisting on using the original Hebrew letters or pronunciation.

13. **Paul Refused to Compel Gentile Converts to Adopt Jewish/Hebrew Cultural Identifiers**
    In Galatians and Acts 15 (the Council of Jerusalem), the Apostles explicitly addressed what was required of Gentile converts. They ruled that Gentiles did not need to become culturally Jewish to follow Jesus. Requiring believers to adopt Hebrew terminology, names, or language patterns contradicts Paul's core message that in Christ *"there is no difference between Jew and Gentile"* (Romans 10:12, Galatians 3:28).

14. **Gentile Converts Kept Their Pagan/Greek Names**
    When Gentiles got saved in the book of Acts and the Epistles, they were not re-baptized with new Hebrew names. They retained their Greek and Roman names—many of which were originally derived from Greek gods or pagan origins:
    - **Apollos** (Acts 18:24) – Named after the Greek god Apollo.
    - **Phoebe** (Romans 16:1) – Meaning "bright/radiant," associated with the Greek goddess Artemis/Diana.
    - **Hermes** (Romans 16:14) – Named directly after the Greek messenger god.
    - **Titus, Epaphroditus, Demetrius, and Aristarchus** all retained their standard Greco-Roman names throughout their Christian service.

15. **Key Church Leadership Titles Were Greco-Roman, Not Hebrew**
    When the Apostles established order and leadership positions in the local churches, they chose standard Greek administrative terms rather than Old Testament Hebrew temple terms:
    - **Episkopos** (*Bishop / Overseer*) – A standard Greek civic term.
    - **Presbyteros** (*Elder*) – A general Greek term for age and governance.
    - **Diakonos** (*Deacon / Servant*) – A common Greek word for a waiter or assistant.
    - **Ekklesia** (*Church / Assembly*) – A secular Greek word for a public assembly or gathering, completely bypassing Hebrew synagogue terminology.
